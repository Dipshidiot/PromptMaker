# STYLE PACK: Grunge Street

## Core Aesthetic
Urban raw energy. Concrete, spray paint, torn posters, and the beauty of things left to weather. Rooted in street photography, graffiti culture, skate culture, and the visual language of cities that work for a living. Honest, gritty, alive.

## Lighting Behavior
Available urban light — sodium streetlamps, harsh noon sun casting hard shadows, grey overcast, fluorescent spill from storefronts. No flattering light. Light is functional, accidental, and perfect. Shadows are deep and unforgiving.

## Color Behavior
Desaturated base with isolated color pops — a single red tag on grey concrete, a yellow hoodie in a grey alley. Base palette: grey, brown, asphalt black, worn white. Accent colors are vivid but singular. Nothing competes; one thing pops.

## Texture Behavior
Concrete grain. Spray paint texture with overspray. Peeling layers of old paste posters. Worn denim and scuffed rubber. Rust. Chain-link fence pattern. The tactile reality of outdoor urban surfaces touched by weather and years.

## Motion Behavior
Kinetic and caught in movement. Skaters mid-trick. A tag being completed. Someone mid-stride. Motion blur acceptable on limbs. The decisive street-photography moment — not posed, not perfect.

## Caption Tone
Short, direct, unpolished. Could be a tag, a flyer headline, or something someone just said on the street. No punctuation optional. Raw and real.

- "built different"
- "concrete was our playground"
- "this city never asked our permission either"

## Transformation Rules

### Realistic
High-contrast street photography aesthetic. Black and white or desaturated color. Real urban environment. Genuine moment captured. Grain pushed. Feels like a photographer who knows these streets personally.

### Stylized
Street art and graffiti illustration hybrid. Bold outlines, spray-paint texture dominant, stencil-style subject rendering. Background is a wall of layered tags and paste-ups. Feels like a live mural.

### Full Crazy
The entire city is a canvas. Buildings are covered floor-to-roof in overlapping murals, every surface tagged, paste-up figures life-size and animated-looking, the street itself is painted. Reality is graffiti all the way down.

## Example Outputs
- **Realistic:** High-contrast black and white shot of a skater mid-ollie over a cracked concrete gap, motion blur on the board, harsh noon shadow, worn city street, grain pushed to 3200 ISO.
- **Stylized:** Stencil-art illustration of the same skater — bold spray-paint edges, vivid yellow on grey, tag-covered wall background, street art typography caption in spray-can style.
- **Full Crazy:** The skater is now a twenty-foot mural figure leaping between buildings, every surface in the city covered in overlapping art, tags spiral into the sky, and the concrete itself has cracked open to reveal more graffiti underneath.
