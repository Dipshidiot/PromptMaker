# TEXT TEXTURE: Comic Halftone

## Identity
The dot-pattern texture of printed comics and vintage newspaper printing. Ben-Day dots visible within the letterforms — circles of color at regular intervals creating the illusion of a solid fill. Graphic, retro, and deeply rooted in print culture.

## Visual Properties

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| **Surface quality** | Regular dot pattern fills the letterforms |
| **Dot size** | Small (subtle) to Large (graphic statement) |
| **Dot spacing** | Uniform — based on chosen dot size |
| **Color rendering** | Dots in the text color on the background; or background-colored dots on text color |
| **Edge behavior** | Dots may clip at edges creating a pixelated-but-intentional boundary |
| **Angle** | Traditional 45° halftone angle for authenticity |

## Best Used With
- Meme Lord (comic book energy)
- Retro Vintage (period printing accuracy)
- Propaganda Parody (poster/print aesthetic)
- Grunge Street (zine-print texture)
- Vaporwave Dream (retro print meets digital surreal)

## Avoid With
- Ghibli Soft (mechanical dots destroy the painted quality)
- Luxury Brand (cheap-print texture contradicts the premium)
- Neon Noir (glow and halftone fight each other)

## Behavior Across Variation Levels

### Realistic
Fine halftone at standard print angle — the dot pattern is visible but the text is still clearly solid at reading distance. Authentic to vintage comic or newspaper printing. Feels like a quality comic panel caption.

### Stylized
Dot size increased to visible graphic element. The halftone IS the design. Background halftone dots may appear in a second color outside the text boundary as a design element. Feels like Roy Lichtenstein.

### Full Crazy
Maximum dot. The letterforms are defined entirely by dots — barely holding together. Some dots have escaped the letters and are orbiting them. The halftone has become chaotic — different dot sizes in different parts of the same letter. Pop art has achieved sentience.
