# TEXT TEXTURE: Grainy

## Identity
Film grain and analog noise applied to the text itself. The letters have the specific texture of type printed, photographed, and not quite perfectly reproduced. Organic, warm, and slightly imperfect — in the best possible way.

## Visual Properties

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| **Surface quality** | Fine to medium noise grain distributed across fill |
| **Noise type** | Film grain — irregular, organic, not digital pixel noise |
| **Edge behavior** | Slightly soft — grain bleeds fractionally at edges |
| **Color rendering** | Slightly uneven — grain creates subtle tonal variation |
| **Intensity options** | Light grain (subtle), Medium grain (visible), Heavy grain (atmospheric) |

## Best Used With
- Retro Vintage (native — period accuracy)
- Neon Noir (adds analog melancholy)
- Dark Academia (suggests age and paper)
- Grunge Street (urban material texture)
- Cottagecore (softens digital sharpness)

## Avoid With
- Cyberpunk Chrome (contradicts the precision aesthetic)
- Luxury Brand (grain reads as damage, not character)
- Corporate Parody (professionalism doesn't grain)

## Behavior Across Variation Levels

### Realistic
Light-to-medium film grain that feels like a practical choice — as if the photograph that contains the text was shot on film. Grain is consistent with the image texture. Unified material quality.

### Stylized
Grain pushed to medium-heavy. Becomes a visible design element — the texture of the type is now part of its personality. Individual grain particles may be visible on large letterforms.

### Full Crazy
Heavy grain pushed to maximum. Letters are barely escaping the noise. The grain IS the text — the letters exist as shapes defined by slightly-less-grain against more-grain. Beautiful degradation.
