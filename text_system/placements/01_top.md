# TEXT PLACEMENT: Top

## Identity
Text anchors to the upper portion of the image. Used to establish context, introduce a subject, or deliver the setup line of a two-part narrative. The image lives below the text and provides the visual punchline or subject.

## Positioning Rules

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| **Vertical anchor** | Top edge of image |
| **Top margin** | 3–6% of image height |
| **Horizontal position** | Center (default) or full-width |
| **Text block width** | 85–95% of image width |
| **Stacking (multi-line)** | Lines stack downward from anchor point |

## Use Cases
- Setup line in a two-part meme (Top + Bottom split)
- Title or header establishing the scene
- Introducing a character, place, or situation
- Captions where the subject needs to be seen first and read second is acceptable

## Style + Texture Compatibility

| Works Well | Use With Caution |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Bold Meme Block | Vintage Noir (may crowd decorative sky) |
| Corporate Parody | Chaotic Burst (may clash with busy upper elements) |
| Vintage Noir (title card) | — |
| Soft Wholesome | — |

## Behavior Across Variation Levels

### Realistic
Clean top margin, clear contrast between text and image. If the image has a busy top region, a semi-transparent dark or light bar is added for legibility. Text is the first thing the eye finds.

### Stylized
Text becomes an integrated design element at the top — may use the image's natural sky, wall, or negative space as the canvas. No background bar needed; style creates sufficient contrast.

### Full Crazy
Text erupts from the top of the frame. May be oversized — approaching 15–20% of image height. Optional motion lines below the text suggest it just arrived. Top edge may be treated as a dynamic element.

## Avoid When
- The subject's face or key element is in the top third of the image (text will obscure it)
- Using Floating placement for the same text (pick one)
- The image deliberately uses the top third as negative space that must remain clear
