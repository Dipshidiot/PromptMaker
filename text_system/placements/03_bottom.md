# TEXT PLACEMENT: Bottom

## Identity
Text anchors to the lower portion of the image. The most common placement for captions, quotes, and punchlines. The image speaks first; the text has the last word. Feels natural, cinematic, and editorial.

## Positioning Rules

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| **Vertical anchor** | Bottom edge of image |
| **Bottom margin** | 3–6% of image height |
| **Horizontal position** | Center (default) or full-width |
| **Text block width** | 85–95% of image width |
| **Stacking (multi-line)** | Lines stack upward from anchor point |

## Use Cases
- Punchline in a two-part meme (paired with Top setup)
- Subtitle or caption text
- Quote attribution
- Photographer/artist credit
- Closing statement on a narrative image

## Style + Texture Compatibility

| Works Well | Use With Caution |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Bold Meme Block | Vintage Noir (elegant at bottom if short) |
| Soft Wholesome | Chaotic Burst (can compete with bottom-weighted images) |
| Corporate Parody | — |
| All styles — the universal safe placement | — |

## Behavior Across Variation Levels

### Realistic
Clean bottom margin. Clear contrast handling — semi-transparent bar, shadow, or natural image space ensures legibility. The gold standard of caption placement. Nothing fancy, nothing wrong.

### Stylized
Bottom text becomes compositionally intentional — the ground of the image, the lower third, is designed with the text in mind. Typography scale and weight are tuned to the visual mass of the image above.

### Full Crazy
Text bursts from the bottom of the frame. May use a large, thick bar of color as an aggressive container. Text is oversized. The bottom of the image is now a stage and the text is performing.

## Avoid When
- The image's most important element is in the bottom third
- Using both Top and Bottom while also requesting Center (pick Split or Center, not both)
- Subtitle-length text that needs more than 3 lines (consider Center instead)
